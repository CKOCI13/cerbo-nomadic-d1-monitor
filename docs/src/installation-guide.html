Installation guide · monitor v2.0

Nomadic D1 DC-DC converter monitor
on your Victron Cerbo GX

Make the cable, set up the Cerbo, and install the monitor. When you're done, the D1's readings appear on the Cerbo screen, in the VRM app and on a web page with live charts.

It only watches. The monitor listens to the D1 and never sends it anything, so the D1 keeps working exactly as it does today, on its own built-in logic.

What you'll see on the Cerbo screen when you're done (drawn from Victron's own screen design, so the real screen may differ slightly).

You'll be able to see

  • 12 V and 48 V side voltage, current and power
  • The D1's temperature, what it's doing (standby, autonomous, derating…) and every alarm, in plain words
  • Charts of the last 24 hours on a web page
  • Everything in the VRM app on your phone

Good to know

  • It takes about 1 hour, including making the cable
  • Nothing changes for the D1. The Cerbo can't send it any commands
  • You don't need to type any commands. The Cerbo Setup helper does it for you

1Get what you need

Gather everything before you start.

For the cable

  • A standard Ethernet patch cable (Cat5e or Cat6), long enough to reach from the Cerbo to the D1
  • Two 3-way lever wire connectors, for example WAGO 221-413. They take 24–12 AWG wire: solid-core Cat5e/Cat6 (about 24 AWG) fits; very thin stranded patch-cable wire may not clamp well, so use solid-core cable, or put a small ferrule on each end
  • Wire cutters and a wire stripper
  • Heat-shrink tubing or electrical tape
  • A multimeter with a continuity (beep) setting and an Ohms setting
  • A VE.Can terminator: the small RJ45 plug that came in the Cerbo box
  • A 120 Ω resistor, ¼ W metal film (1 %), for the D1 end. Measure it before use: 115–125 Ω

For the software

  • A Windows PC on the same network (Wi-Fi or Ethernet) as the Cerbo
  • The project folder downloaded from GitHub, which contains Cerbo Setup.bat and the dbus-nomadic-dcdc folder
  • The Cerbo running Venus OS v3.80 or newer. Step 3 shows you how to check

Safety

Before you work on the D1's wiring, switch off its 12 V supply (KL30/KL15) and follow the D1 manual. The CAN cable only carries data, but it ends next to the D1's 48 V and 12 V power terminals.

2Make the cable

The Cerbo uses RJ45 sockets, the same plug as network cables. Take a ready-made network cable, keep the plug on one end for the Cerbo, and cut off the other end. You only need 2 of its 8 wires: the brown pair. The D1 already has labelled wires coming out of its signal plug (CAN_H, CAN_L and its two voltage wires, KL15 and KL30), so you join the network cable to its CAN_H and CAN_L wires with two lever wire connectors, and put the 120 Ω resistor between the two connectors. No ground wire is needed.

The whole connection

Blue: the network cable you make. The two lever connectors join it to the D1's CAN_H and CAN_L wires, with the required 120 Ω resistor between them. Red: the D1's own 12 V wires (KL30 and KL15). If your D1 already works today, they're already in place.

The two lever connectors

Each connector joins three things: one D1 wire, one network-cable wire and one leg of the resistor. The resistor bridges the two connectors, so it sits across CAN_H and CAN_L at the D1.

Which wire goes where

Pins 7 and 8 are the brown pair (twisted together), which suits CAN well. Everything else, including white/green (pin 3), is not used.

WireRJ45 pin (Cerbo)Goes to
white/brown7Lever connector A, with the D1's wire labelled CAN_H

Sources: Victron's VE.Can to CAN-bus BMS cables manual (Cerbo side: pin 7 CAN-H, pin 8 CAN-L) and the D1 Bidirectional DC-DC Converter Manual, Rev D.

Make it

2a
Cut off one plug. Keep the other plug for the Cerbo. Strip about 5 cm (2 in) of the grey outer jacket.
2b
Check the wires with a multimeter on the continuity (beep) setting. Touch one probe to a gold contact on the plug and the other to a wire:
  • Pin 7 should beep with white/brown
  • Pin 8 should beep with brown
2c
Insulate the 6 wires you don't need, including white/green. Cut them short and cover each one separately with heat-shrink or tape, so none can touch anything.
2d
Join the wires with the two lever connectors, with the D1 switched off. Strip each wire 11 mm (the strip gauge on the connector's side), lift the lever, push the wire fully in, and close the lever.
  • Connector A (CAN-H): the D1's wire labelled CAN_H, the white/brown wire, and one leg of the resistor
  • Connector B (CAN-L): the D1's wire labelled CAN_L, the brown wire, and the other leg of the resistor
Slide heat-shrink over both resistor legs so they can't touch each other or anything metal. Keep the brown pair twisted to within a few centimetres of the connectors, and tie the connectors and cable to something solid (or put them in a small box), so vibration can't pull on them.
2e
No ground wire. CAN works on the difference between CAN_H and CAN_L. The Cerbo and the D1 already share the van's negative through their power wiring, so a separate CAN ground isn't needed. Leaving it off also avoids a thin wire running in parallel with the main negative cables (the Cerbo's VE.Can 2 port is not isolated). Once everything runs, step 5c includes a quick check of the ground difference.
Never connect any of the network-cable wires to the D1's HV+ or LV+ terminals. They only join the D1's labelled CAN_H and CAN_L wires.
2f
Check the D1's two voltage wires (KL30 and KL15). The D1 needs both to work at all, and without them it's also silent on CAN. If your D1 already works today, they're already in place: just check them. On the Grand Design LTV1 both are joined to one always-on, fused 12 V wire, which keeps the D1 always enabled.
WireConnect to
KL30A permanent 12 V supply (9–16 V, up to 2 A) through a 3 A fuse close to the 12 V source
KL15A 12 V "enable" signal (8 V or more = on). Link it to KL30 to keep the D1 always enabled, or wire it through a switch, ignition or relay. The manual (Rev D) fuses it at 1 A when it has its own wire
2g
Check the resistor. With the RJ45 plug not yet in the Cerbo, measure between pins 7 and 8 of the RJ45 plug (or the white/brown and brown wires). It should read about 120 Ω. If it reads open, a wire isn't fully in its lever connector.
2h
Final check: plug the RJ45 into the Cerbo port you'll use, with the VE.Can terminator in that port's other socket. With everything switched off, measure between white/brown and brown again:
You measureMeaning
About 60 ΩCorrect: both ends are terminated
About 120 ΩThe VE.Can terminator isn't in the Cerbo port's other socket, or the cable is in a different port
Very high or "OL"The resistor at the D1 isn't connected, or a wire isn't fully in its lever connector. Re-check steps 2d and 2g

3Set up the Cerbo

Do this on the Cerbo's touchscreen, or remotely with Remote Console in VRM. Every item below is found from the Settings menu.

3a
Check the firmware version. Go to Settings › General › Firmware. It needs to be v3.80 or newer. If it's older, use Online updates on the same page to update first.
Then go to Settings › General › Modification checks and make sure modifications are enabled (that's the default). If they are off, Venus OS switches off the start-up script and the driver disappears after the next restart.
3b
Write down the Cerbo's IP address. Go to Settings › Connectivity › Ethernet (or Wi-Fi) and read the IP address.
IP address:
3c
Choose the CAN port and speed. Use the Cerbo's second CAN port, with nothing else plugged into it. The labels next to the ports tell you which Cerbo you have:
  • Cerbo GX MK2 (ports labeled VE.Can 1 and VE.Can 2): go to Settings › Connectivity › VE.Can port 2 (some firmware still calls it BMS-Can port) and set CAN-bus profile to VE.Can (250 kbit/s) (the default). The D1 talks at either 250 or 500 kbit/s. Start with 250. Step 5c shows each port's speed, and tells you if the D1 is talking at the other speed. Then change the profile to CAN-bus BMS LV (500 kbit/s) (older firmware: CAN-bus BMS (500 kbit/s)).
    Victron's own messages on that port use a different kind of CAN ID (29-bit) from the D1's (11-bit), so they don't get in each other's way. You may see the profile Up, but no services mentioned elsewhere: the Cerbo screen no longer lets you choose it, and it isn't needed.
  • Original Cerbo GX (second port labeled BMS-Can): go to Settings › Connectivity › BMS-Can port. This port only runs at 500 kbit/s, so leave it on CAN-bus BMS (500 kbit/s). It works only if the D1 talks at 500 kbit/s. If step 5c reports bus errors on it, the D1 is at 250 kbit/s: use the VE.Can port (if nothing else is on it) with VE.Can (250 kbit/s) instead.
3d
Let your PC connect to the Cerbo (this switches on SSH):
  1. Go to Settings › General › Access & Security › Access level and choose User & Installer. The password is ZZZ.
  2. Still on the Access & Security page, drag the list down with your finger and hold it for about 5 seconds (on Remote Console with a keyboard: hold the right-arrow key). The access level changes to Superuser.
  3. Tap Root password and choose a password (at least 8 characters).
    Root password:
  4. Switch Enable SSH on LAN to on.

4Connect everything

4a
Plug the cable's RJ45 plug into the CAN port you chose in 3c, with the VE.Can terminator in the port's other socket (as in step 2h).
4b
Plug the D1's signal plug back in and switch its 12 V supply (KL30/KL15) back on.
Nothing changes for the D1, now or later. The monitor can only listen: it has no way to send the D1 a command.

5Install the monitor with Cerbo Setup

5a
Open the helper. In the project folder, double-click Cerbo Setup.bat. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info and then Run anyway.
5b
Type the Cerbo's IP address from step 3b and press Enter. The helper remembers it for next time, and then shows its menu:

    Cerbo address: 192.168.1.50

  ===========================================================

    2  Find which Cerbo port the converter is on
    3  Watch live readings from the converter
    4  Install or update the monitor on the Cerbo
    5  Show the monitor log

    U  Make a USB-stick installer (no network needed)

    9  Try it with a pretend converter (simulation)

    6  Change the Cerbo address
    7  Fix "REMOTE HOST IDENTIFICATION HAS CHANGED"
    8  Uninstall the monitor from the Cerbo
    0  Exit

  Type a number and press Enter: _
Want to see it first? Option 9 runs everything with a pretend D1 and no CAN bus at all (see the Safe Test Plan, docs/safe-test-plan.pdf).
The password prompt. Every option that talks to the Cerbo asks for the root password from step 3d. Nothing appears on screen while you type it. That's normal: type it and press Enter.
5c
Type 2: find the converter. The helper copies the monitor to the Cerbo and listens on every CAN port for 3 seconds. You should see something like this:
Venus OS v3.80 OK
Listening 3 s on each CAN port (nothing is transmitted)...
  can0    250 kbit/s  error-active  nothing from the converter
  can1    250 kbit/s  error-active  CONVERTER FOUND (391 frames)
The converter is on can1. config.ini has interface = auto, so the driver finds it there by itself. Good.

Port names differ between Cerbo models (can1, or vecan1 on the MK2). That's fine: with interface = auto the driver uses whichever port the D1 is on. If the D1 is set to the other speed, you'll see bus errors instead:

  can1    250 kbit/s  error-passive nothing decoded, but 288 bus errors: talking at a different speed?
Something is talking on can1 at a different speed. On the Cerbo, set that port's CAN-bus
profile to "CAN-bus BMS LV (500 kbit/s)" and run this again.

Do what it says (step 3c), then type 2 again. If nothing is heard at all, see Troubleshooting, and check the KL30/KL15 supply first.

Ground check (no CAN ground wire). Later, with the D1 working hard, measure between the Cerbo's negative terminal and the D1's GND terminal. A few tenths of a volt is normal. More than about 1 V points to a poor negative connection: fix that first. If the Cerbo ever can't hear the D1 although the wiring and the 60 Ω check are right, try a wire from RJ45 pin 3 (white/green) to the D1's GND as a test.
5d
Type 1: edit the settings. Notepad opens config.ini. Check these lines, then press Ctrl+S to save and close Notepad:
LineWhat to put
interface = autoLeave as auto: the driver finds the D1's CAN port by itself
custom_name = DC-DC D1The name shown on the Cerbo and in VRM
password =Optional: a password for the web page. Without one, anyone on your network can see the readings (but can't change anything)
switch_pane = yesLeave as yes to get the cards on the Cerbo screen and in VRM
5e
Type 3: watch the live readings (optional, but a good check). The voltages should match what a meter shows on the D1's terminals. To stop, press Ctrl+C, then type N and press Enter.

5f
Type 4: install. After about 10 seconds you should see:

Done! It now starts by itself after every reboot and firmware update.

No network to the Cerbo? Install from a USB stick instead

Venus OS can install add-ons from a USB stick or microSD card by itself, with no SSH or password. Do this instead of 5c–5f:

U1
Get the file. Download venus-data-nomadic-d1-monitor.tar.gz from the latest release (https://github.com/CKOCI13/cerbo-nomadic-d1-monitor/releases/latest). It has the standard settings, with interface = auto, so there's nothing to edit. To change settings first, edit config.ini (Cerbo Setup option 1) and type U to make your own copy of the file. On a Mac, download it with Chrome or Firefox: Safari can unpack it into a .tar file, which the Cerbo ignores.
U2
Copy it to the top folder of a USB stick or SD card (FAT32), not inside another folder. Put only one venus-data file on the stick. If your browser saved it as venus-data-nomadic-d1-monitor (1).tar.gz, rename it back to venus-data-nomadic-d1-monitor.tar.gz: the Cerbo skips names with spaces.
U3
Put the stick in the Cerbo and restart it (Settings › General › Reboot, or switch it off and on). The Cerbo unpacks the file and installs the monitor while it starts.
U4
Wait about 2 minutes, then take the stick out. The Cerbo renames the file on the stick to ...tar.gz.installed, so it never installs twice. Then open the switch pane: the DC-DC D1 cards should be there.
Without SSH you can't run "find the converter" (5c). If the Status card says No communication, check the CAN port you chose in 3c, its speed (250 / 500 kbit/s) and the wiring, and restart the Cerbo. To install again, use a fresh file (U1).
Upgrading from the old control version (1.x)? Installing this monitor (option 4) replaces it. If the old version was in Off, Buck or Boost, its commands stop when the monitor starts, and nobody knows yet whether the D1 then goes back to its own logic or keeps the last command. So first switch the old version to Automatic (it then sends nothing), check the D1 behaves as it does on its own, and only then install. If it seems stuck, switch KL15 off and on to restart its controller (if KL15 is joined to KL30, as on the LTV1, pull that wire's fuse for a few seconds).

Tap the switches icon at the top left of the screen. Three cards appear (swipe if your screen shows fewer at once):

To rename, move or hide any item, go to Settings › Devices › DC-DC D1. A hidden Precharge status item is there too (the D1 manual doesn't use precharge). The D1 also appears under Settings › Devices as a DC-DC converter, with its 12 V side as the output and its 48 V side as the input.

In the VRM app and portal

The same cards appear in the Switches widget in the VRM app, and in the Switches panel on the VRM portal dashboard. The Cerbo must be online with VRM. Nothing needs to be changed in VRM's access settings: there is nothing to control.

The picture is an illustration. VRM draws its own version of these cards, so the layout may differ a little.

On a web page, with charts

On any phone, tablet or computer on the same network, open:

http://<Cerbo IP>:8088

It shows the live readings, which way the energy is flowing, every alarm, the raw status bits, and charts of the last 10 minutes to 24 hours. Point at a chart to read the values at that moment. The history starts again when the monitor restarts.

When something is wrong

An example: the D1 is hot, so it's limiting its power (derating). The alarm shows the most serious problem first.

Reading the numbers

On its own, the D1 bucks (charges the 12 V side from 48 V) when the 12 V side drops below 12.5 V, as long as the 48 V side is 41–58 V, and fills it back up to about 13.4 V. It boosts (pushes 12 V energy up to 48 V) when the 12 V side is 13.5–16 V, until it falls below 12.3 V. In between it waits in Standby, with no current.
